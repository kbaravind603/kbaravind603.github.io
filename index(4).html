01

Protein engineering · flagship wet-lab project

Engineering and characterising a brighter green fluorescent protein

End-to-end experimental workflow

Investigated whether F64L and S65T substitutions could improve GFP folding and fluorescence. The work connected DNA manipulation, recombinant expression, protein purification, orthogonal identity confirmation and functional measurement.

Plasmid preparation→Restriction & ligation→Site-directed mutagenesis→Expression→Ni-NTA purification→Fluorimetry

DNA engineering

Plasmid extraction, restriction digestion, gel purification, ligation, transformation, colony PCR, site-directed mutagenesis and sequence alignment.

Protein production

Auto-induction in E. coli BL21(DE3), cell lysis, soluble/insoluble fractionation and Ni-NTA affinity purification.

Characterisation

SDS-PAGE, Western blot, NanoDrop quantification, LC-ESI-MS and matched-concentration fluorimetry.

Key result~3.4× higher maximum fluorescence intensity

The mutation strategy produced a functionally improved recombinant protein supported by sequence, gel-based, immunochemical, mass and fluorescence evidence.

02

Immunopharmacology · in vivo model

Post-infection immune stimulation in an E. coli-challenged zebrafish model

Experimental study with computational rationale

Examined an immune stimulant administered after bacterial challenge rather than prophylactically. The project combined microbial culture, an adult zebrafish infection model, dissection, qRT-PCR, survival observations and a computationally supported receptor hypothesis.

Wet-lab model

Prepared bacterial culture, established post-challenge treatment groups, verified intestinal colonisation by plating and monitored survival and behaviour.

Molecular readout

Performed RNA isolation, cDNA synthesis and SYBR Green qRT-PCR using IL-1β as an immune marker, β-actin as reference and 16S rRNA as a bacterial marker.

Scientific judgement

The findings supported immune activation and improved survival, while the design did not permit a definitive claim of bacterial clearance.

Observed survival

60.0%infection only
73.3%low dose
86.7%high dose
Key resultDose-associated improvement in survival with measurable immune activation

The high-dose group showed 86.7% survival and a 2.83-fold increase in IL-1β expression, with limitations reported transparently.

03

Computational support for early discovery

Structure-based lead optimisation against a confidential bacterial target

Target and compound identities withheld

Used molecular modelling to support a broader early-discovery programme. The project demonstrates how I use computational methods to generate and prioritise experimentally testable hypotheses rather than treating docking scores as experimental evidence.

Target preparation→Binding-site assessment→Ligand preparation→HTVS / SP / XP→SAR design→ADME & toxicity triage

Hypothesis generation

Prepared the target, assessed candidate binding regions and generated docking grids before processing a ligand set through LigPrep and staged Glide docking.

Optimisation reasoning

Designed systematic substitutions, re-docked each analogue and compared poses using interaction quality, pocket complementarity and maximum-common-substructure RMSD.

Prioritisation

Stopped design once favourable structural changes were understood, then shortlisted compounds using predicted physicochemical, ADME and toxicological properties.

Role of modellingsupport experimental prioritisation and explain structure–activity trends
Evidence integratedpose quality, interactions, RMSD, docking score and predicted developability
Scientific boundarypredictions framed as hypotheses requiring experimental validation
